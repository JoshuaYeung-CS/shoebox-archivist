# Story rules

Each album gets one short story note. Its job is to make the album worth opening for a family member who was not there, without saying anything the prints do not support.

## What a story may use

Only the album facts supplied to the document expert:

- the approved date and its confidence level;
- captions copied verbatim from the prints and album pages;
- the people the user has named in Photos who appear in the album;
- the scene descriptions from step 3;
- anything the user typed while reviewing the plan.

## What a story must not do

- Invent events, feelings, relationships, ages, names or places.
- State a date more precisely than its level. A Year album says "in 1974", a Decade album says "sometime in the 1980s".
- Describe or speculate about unnamed people beyond "a group of friends" or "two children" when the scene description says so.
- Mention the scanning, the Skill, the NAS or the evidence method.

## Shape

- At most 120 words, in the user reply language.
- First line: the album name as a heading.
- One paragraph. If a caption exists, quote the best one exactly.
- Last line: "Date: 1974-08 (Month, from the back of print 3)" style provenance, so later readers know how sure the date is.

## Checks before showing a draft

The parent checks each draft against the supplied facts before showing it:

1. Every name in the draft is in the named-people list.
2. Every place in the draft appears in a caption or in the user text.
3. The date wording matches the confidence level.
4. The word count is 120 or less.

A draft that fails a check is not shown. Ask the document expert once for a corrected draft with the failed check named; if it fails again, show the facts list instead of a story and say why.

## Saving

Stories are shown together for review (`waiting_user`). Only approved text is saved, exactly as approved, one Markdown note per album in the story folder, named after the album. If a note with that name already exists from an earlier run, the ledger shows it; ask whether to keep the old one or save the new one with a run suffix.
